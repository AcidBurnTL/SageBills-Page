5 Apps Every European Renter Should Have on Their iPhone

Renting in a European city in 2026 means managing a surprising amount of administrative complexity: utility bills in a language you might not fully read, building maintenance requests that go unanswered, rental contracts with clauses that vary dramatically by country, and a landlord who may or may not respond promptly. These five iPhone apps cover the core workflow without redundancy.

1. SageBills — Utility Bill Tracking

The most practical utility tracker built specifically for the European context. SageBills supports all standard European utility categories, scans invoices via OCR, and keeps everything on-device with iCloud backup. For renters, the budget tracking features are particularly useful for anticipating the autumn-to-winter heating spike and avoiding bill shock. Free, no account required.

2. SageDocs — Rental Document Vault

Every renter accumulates a small archive of high-stakes documents: the rental contract, the move-in inventory and photos, the move-out condition report, copies of utility welcome packets, insurance policies, and ID scans you may need to send the landlord. These belong in something built for sensitive documents — not in a notes app, not in a generic cloud drive, and certainly not in your Photos library where they get backed up to whatever you've enabled.

SageDocs is the document vault built for exactly this. AES-256-GCM encryption on every attachment, biometric/PIN protection, encrypted iCloud sync — meaning the only person who can decrypt your rental contract is you. Categories cover identity, insurance, contracts, and medical, and you can set expiry reminders for things like rental term end dates so you get a notification before you need to act. For correspondence threads, ongoing notes, or password storage, pair it with whatever you already use (email, a notes app, a password manager); SageDocs handles the document layer.

3. Revolut or Wise — Cross-Border Payments

If you pay rent to a landlord in a different country, or receive salary in one currency and pay bills in another, a multi-currency account is essential. Both Revolut and Wise offer real exchange rates with minimal fees, standing orders, and instant notifications. For the specific use case of cross-border rent and utility payments, they reduce friction significantly.

4. Google Translate Camera Mode — Invoice Reading

For renters who moved from another country, reading utility invoices in Romanian, Polish, or Hungarian can be genuinely difficult. Google Translate’s live camera mode overlays English text in real time. Point it at any invoice and it gives you a readable version instantly — good enough to tell whether a bill is routine or requires attention.

5. Fixly or TaskRabbit — Maintenance Requests

When the landlord is slow to respond to maintenance issues, having a platform-vetted tradesperson you can book directly — and subsequently invoice the landlord for — gives you leverage and resolution. Fixly operates in Romania and Poland; TaskRabbit has coverage in major CEE cities. Document everything through the platform for a clean paper trail.
