Understanding Your Romanian Electricity Bill: A Complete Guide

Romanian electricity invoices — whether from Enel (now Cez), Electrica, or one of the competitive market suppliers — pack a surprising amount of information into a single page. Most of it is never explained, and the line items are frequently presented in terminology that differs between providers. This guide decodes the standard invoice structure so you know exactly what you are paying for.

The Two Fundamental Components

Every Romanian electricity bill is composed of two broad categories: the supply component (energia electrica activa) and the network component (servicii de retea). The supply component covers the actual electricity you consume, priced per kWh at whatever tariff you are on. The network component covers the physical infrastructure — the wires, transformers, and grid that deliver electricity to your home — and is paid to the local distribution operator regardless of who your supply provider is.

Key Line Items Explained

Tarif reglementat / Tarif concurential: This tells you whether you are on the regulated universal service tariff (set by ANRE, the energy regulator) or a competitive market tariff (negotiated with a supplier). The regulated tariff is not always cheaper — compare both annually.

Capacitate rezervata (Reserved Capacity): A fixed monthly charge based on the contracted power capacity at your meter, measured in kW. You pay this even if you use zero electricity. Households can request a capacity reduction if their actual peak usage is consistently below the contracted level — many never do.

Certificate verzi (Green Certificates): A renewable energy support levy applied per kWh consumed. The rate changes annually via ANRE regulation. In 2025–2026 it is approximately 0.041 RON/kWh.

Servicii de sistem (System Services): A small per-kWh charge that funds the national grid balancing authority (Transelectrica). Non-negotiable and applied uniformly across all customers.

Acciza (Excise Duty): A mandatory EU excise tax on electricity consumption. In Romania it is a fixed rate per MWh, currently set at the EU minimum threshold.

Estimated vs. Actual Reads

Enel and Electrica both use estimated meter reads in months when a physical read is not performed. Estimated months are marked on the invoice, usually with an asterisk or the label estimat. When the next actual read occurs, the difference is applied to that bill — which can cause unexpected spikes. You can avoid this entirely by submitting your own meter reads via the provider app before the billing cutoff date each month.

How to Spot an Error

Compare your kWh consumption month-on-month. A typical two-person apartment in Romania uses 120–200 kWh in summer and 200–350 kWh in winter. If any month is more than 40% above your seasonal norm, check whether it is marked as estimated or if it contains a correction from a prior period.
